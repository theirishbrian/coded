# Product feedback

Coded uses a public GitHub issue form to learn whether its public-profile snapshot
helps with a real task. The application links to the form from its shared footer.
Coded does not receive or store a separate form submission, email address or private
profile data.

## Before submitting

Feedback issues are public. Contributors must not include private, sensitive or
confidential information. The form asks for a self-selected perspective, intended
use, what helped and what remained unclear or missing. It does not ask for contact
details, a private repository, an employer or a rating of a developer.

Security vulnerabilities do not belong in feedback issues. Report them privately
through the process in [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md).

## Weekly review

Review new feedback alongside the aggregate signals in [ANALYTICS.md](ANALYTICS.md):

1. Record the date range and links to the public feedback issues reviewed.
2. Group repeated observations by the task people were trying to complete.
3. Separate what a contributor said from an inference about why it happened.
4. Note contradicting feedback and missing perspectives rather than averaging them away.
5. Change scope only when the evidence supports a bounded, testable improvement.

A comment, pageview or self-selected role is an observation. It is not proof of
demand, retention, willingness to pay, developer ability or recruiter adoption.
Avoid copying contributor identities into planning documents when a link to the
public issue is sufficient. Remove sensitive material from public view if it is
submitted accidentally and follow the security policy where appropriate.

## Trust boundary

Feedback can identify useful context or missing explanations, but it must not be
converted into an opaque developer score. Any later contribution or ranking work
still requires published inputs, exclusions, missing-data behaviour and reproducible
rules. Product decisions should cite the observations that motivated them and keep
uncertainty visible.
