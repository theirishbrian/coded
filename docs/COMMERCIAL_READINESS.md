# Commercial readiness after v0.1

Coded should leave v0.1 as a small, credible product and a maintainable asset.
The first release proves one journey: enter a public personal GitHub username and
receive an honest, shareable snapshot of the account's public profile, owned public
repositories and reported primary languages.

## Evidence before expansion

Post-release decisions should use observed behaviour rather than feature count.
Measure visits, successful lookups, repeat use, shared profile URLs and conversion
to any future paid offer. Define each event before collecting it, avoid private
repository data, and document retention and privacy choices before analytics ship.

Interview or observe real developers and recruiters to learn whether the current
snapshot answers a useful question. Treat proposed targets for users, activity or
revenue as decision gates and hypotheses, not forecasts or claims of traction.
Marketplace asking prices are not evidence of completed sales or valuation.

## Asset quality

Keep the assets a future buyer or maintainer can verify:

- a focused brand and product promise;
- the domain and deployment configuration, when selected;
- clean issue, commit, PR, test and release history;
- documented architecture, data limits, security reporting and rollback;
- reproducible builds with sensible, reviewed dependencies;
- privacy-conscious analytics with definitions and exportable evidence;
- real user, engagement and revenue records if they are earned; and
- clear ownership and licensing for code, content and third-party services.

The MIT licence makes the source reusable, but it does not by itself establish
product demand or business value. Distribution, trust, active users and revenue
evidence matter more than adding a long speculative feature list.

## Product sequence

v0.1 is published and verified without activity feeds or ranking. The next steps are:

1. Establish a small acquisition loop and privacy-conscious product analytics.
2. Validate the public-profile use case with real users and document findings.
3. Scope the smallest improvement supported by that evidence.
4. Test monetisation only after the free product shows repeat value.

Possible later work includes profile ownership, selected-project presentation,
custom URLs, resume export, profile analytics and recruiter views. Activity and
contribution views need documented coverage. Any ranking must remain transparent,
explainable and avoid presenting a single score as developer ability.

Do not prepare a marketplace listing merely because development is complete.
A listing decision should follow a due-diligence audit of ownership, licence,
deployment, analytics, users, revenue, operating costs and transfer instructions.
